A website audit is a full health check of everything that affects how a site performs: page speed, security, SEO basics, usability, content quality, and compliance. It is broader than an SEO audit, because a site can rank fine and still lose customers to a slow load, a broken form, or an expired SSL certificate. This is the audit we run before recommending any deeper specialist work for a client at PipeRocket Digital.

It is interactive. Tick each item as you complete it, your progress saves in your browser, and you can download the whole thing as a PDF or Excel.

## How to use this checklist

Work top to bottom in one pass, noting findings as you go rather than fixing them mid-audit. Once you have surfaced every issue, score it by impact and effort, then decide which areas need a deeper, specialist pass, an SEO audit, a technical crawl, or a security review, rather than trying to fix everything from this single list.

## Confirm access and set a baseline

Before touching anything, confirm you have access to Search Console, GA4, Bing Webmaster Tools, and CMS or hosting admin. Verify that conversions and key events are actually firing in GA4, not just installed, and confirm uptime monitoring exists and alerts the right person. Record a baseline of current traffic, rankings, and Core Web Vitals scores so you have something to measure the fix against.

## Check performance and Core Web Vitals

Slow pages lose visitors before they ever judge your content. Test Core Web Vitals on mobile in PageSpeed Insights, compress and correctly size every image, and confirm a CDN and browser caching are switched on. Strip out unused scripts, plugins, and render-blocking resources, since these are usually the real cause of a slow homepage.

## Audit security and technical hygiene

Confirm HTTPS is enforced sitewide with no mixed-content warnings, and check the SSL certificate is valid and set to auto-renew rather than something someone has to remember. Update CMS core, plugins, and themes to current versions, scan for malware and unfamiliar admin users, and confirm a recent, tested backup actually exists, not just a setting that says one should.

## Cover the SEO fundamentals

This is a lighter pass than a full SEO audit, just enough to catch anything obviously broken. Confirm the right pages are indexed and nothing important is accidentally noindexed, review robots.txt and the XML sitemap for mistakes, and find and fix broken links and 404s. Check that every page has a unique title tag and meta description, and confirm the site is mobile-friendly throughout.

## Test UX, accessibility and conversion

Walk your primary conversion path end to end on both mobile and desktop, the way a real visitor would. Confirm the navigation and information architecture make sense to someone who has never seen the site before, and test that every form actually submits and routes to the right place. Check color contrast, image alt text, and keyboard navigation, since these are also the checks that catch the accessibility issues most likely to turn into a complaint.

## Review content and compliance

Inventory your pages and flag anything outdated, thin, or duplicated. Confirm contact details, business information, and hours are current, since these are the details visitors and Google both use to judge trust. Check that a privacy policy and a cookie or consent banner are in place and accurate for where your visitors are.

## Prioritise and turn it into action

An audit only matters if it changes what your team does next. Score every finding by impact and effort, turn the list into an owner-assigned action plan with deadlines, and flag anything that needs a deeper specialist pass, SEO, technical, or security, rather than a quick fix. Schedule the next full audit; twice a year is a sensible default for most sites.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a website audit checklist?

A website audit checklist is a structured list of checks across every area that affects a site's performance: page speed, security, SEO fundamentals, usability, content quality, and compliance. It is broader than an SEO audit, which focuses specifically on organic search factors.

### How often should I audit my website?

Run a full website audit twice a year for most sites, and always after a major redesign, replatform, or migration. Between full audits, keep an eye on uptime, Core Web Vitals, and Search Console coverage so regressions get caught early.

### What is the difference between a website audit and an SEO audit?

A website audit covers the whole site: performance, security, SEO basics, UX, content, and compliance. An SEO audit goes much deeper on one of those areas, indexation, on-page, technical SEO, content, and backlinks, to find everything affecting organic rankings specifically. Run the broader audit first, then the SEO audit if search performance needs closer attention.

### What tools do I need for a website audit?

At minimum: Google Search Console and GA4 (free), PageSpeed Insights for performance, an SSL checker, and a crawler like Screaming Frog for broken links and indexation. A security scanner and an accessibility checker (such as WAVE) cover the remaining sections.

### What should I fix first after a website audit?

Fix anything that blocks a sale or a signup first: a broken form, a security warning in the browser, or a page that fails to load on mobile. After that, work through the highest-impact, lowest-effort items, since those move the needle fastest without a large project behind them.
