A new website SEO checklist covers everything a brand-new site needs before, during, and just after launch: keyword-to-page planning, crawlable foundations, tracking setup, and the first steps toward earning authority a new domain does not have yet. This is different from migrating an existing site, there is no traffic to protect, only a clean slate to set up correctly. This is the sequence we run when a client at PipeRocket Digital launches a new SaaS site.

It is interactive. Tick each item as you complete it, your progress saves in your browser, and you can download the whole thing as a PDF or Excel to hand to developers.

## How to use this checklist

Work through it in the order the phases appear, planning and building sections before launch, launch-day items at the moment you go live, and the last section in the weeks after. Skipping the planning phase is the most common mistake, since fixing a keyword-to-page mismatch after launch means reworking pages that are already indexed.

## Plan before you build

Map every target keyword to a specific planned page before development starts, so you are not retrofitting SEO onto a finished site. Sketch the information architecture and URL structure at the same time, and check the live SERP for your core keywords to confirm what you are actually competing against. If the domain is not brand new, confirm it has no penalty or spam history first.

## Build SEO-friendly foundations

Use short, readable, keyword-aware URLs sitewide from the first page you build. Confirm the site is mobile-first, responsive, and fast by default rather than optimised after the fact, and make sure content renders in HTML rather than JavaScript-only, so it stays reliably crawlable. Serve the whole site on HTTPS from day one.

## Set up tracking and verification

Verify the domain in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools before launch, not after. Install GA4 and confirm the conversions that matter are actually tracked, generate an XML sitemap and check it for errors, and review robots.txt so nothing important gets accidentally blocked.

## Nail on-page basics on every page

Every page needs a unique title tag and meta description, one clear H1, and a logical heading structure, built in from the start rather than patched later. Add descriptive alt text to every image, and build an internal linking plan that connects every page to a relevant hub so authority flows through the site instead of pooling on the homepage.

## Run pre-launch QA

Remove the staging noindex tag and confirm the live site is actually crawlable, since this is the single most common launch-day mistake. Test every form, link, and CTA on mobile and desktop, run a crawl to catch broken links or redirect errors, and confirm structured data validates in the Rich Results Test.

## Handle launch day

Submit the XML sitemap in Search Console the moment the site goes live, and request indexing on your most important pages rather than waiting for Google to find them. Confirm robots.txt is not blocking anything post-launch, since a leftover staging rule is easy to miss, and set up uptime and crawl-error monitoring from day one.

## Build initial authority post-launch

A new domain starts with zero authority, so plan for that. Publish a content and internal-linking cadence for the first 90 days, claim the key profiles and directories relevant to your niche, and start earning a small number of genuine, relevant backlinks rather than chasing volume. Check Search Console weekly; new domains take longer to fully index and rank than an established site would.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a new website SEO checklist?

A new website SEO checklist is the set of steps that gets a brand-new site's SEO right from the start: keyword-to-page planning, crawlable technical foundations, tracking setup, on-page basics, and the initial work to start earning authority once the site is live.

### How long does it take a new website to start ranking?

Most new domains need three to six months before meaningful organic traffic arrives, and competitive terms can take longer. Getting the planning, technical foundations, and on-page basics right from launch shortens that window; a site that needs rework after indexing starts from further behind.

### What is the difference between a new website SEO checklist and a site migration checklist?

A new website checklist starts from a blank slate, there is no existing traffic or rankings to protect. A site migration checklist is for an existing site changing domain, platform, or structure, where the priority is a clean redirect map so you keep the rankings you already earned.

### Do I need backlinks before I launch a new website?

No. Focus pre-launch effort on the technical and on-page foundations instead. Backlink building genuinely matters, but it belongs in the weeks after launch, once the site is indexed and there is something live worth linking to.

### What is the biggest SEO mistake when launching a new website?

Leaving the staging noindex tag in place at launch, or skipping keyword-to-page planning until after the site is built. Both are cheap to fix before launch and expensive to fix afterward, since fixing them post-launch often means reworking pages Google has already crawled and indexed.
