An SEO SOP (standard operating procedure) is a simple, step-by-step plan that one SEO team member follows for every client, from the first day right through to the weekly report. A good one makes sure your search work brings in real demos and leads, not just traffic that looks nice but never buys. This playbook is written as one person's routine, so anyone on the team can pick it up and run it.

Most SEO checklists give you a pile of tips with no order, so no one knows what to do first. This one is different. It is the exact 13-step workflow we follow at PipeRocket Digital, in the right order, so you always know what comes next.

## How to use this SOP

Think of the 13 phases as one timeline. The first few phases set things up: onboarding, access, a technical check, tracking, and research. Then you build the pages that bring in demos, add supporting content and links, work on AI search, and report every week. Each phase shows when it happens, so you can plan your weeks. Tick each point off as you finish it (your progress saves in your browser), and download the whole thing as a PDF or Excel to keep beside you.

## The workflow in simple points (with an example)

Here is the whole routine in plain points, the way one SEO team member would run it. To make it real, we follow a made-up client called Flowbase, a B2B SaaS that sells project management software to marketing agencies and competes with bigger tools like Asana and Monday.

## Phase 1: Onboarding and discovery
**When:** Week 1.

- Send the client an onboarding sheet. Learn who buys the tool, who the rivals are, the deal size, the industries, and what makes them different. (Flowbase sells to agencies and competes with Asana.)
- Agree on one main goal: booked demos, not just visits.
- Set the expectation that month one is setup, so no one worries when rankings have not moved.
- Save it all. It feeds the [SaaS SEO strategy](/checklists/saas-seo-strategy-checklist/) you build next.

## Phase 2: Access and tooling
**When:** Week 1.

- Collect every login: Search Console, GA4, Clarity, the CRM (Flowbase uses HubSpot), Tag Manager, and access to edit the website.
- Check each login actually works before you start.
- Find out who can edit the site. If the client can, like Flowbase, fixes go live in days, not weeks.
- Save the logins safely and connect the tools to your [reporting setup](/checklists/saas-seo-reporting-checklist/).

## Phase 3: Technical audit
**When:** Weeks 1-2.

- Do a quick outside check first, then a deep check once the logins arrive.
- Look for pages that only load their text after JavaScript runs, because Google can miss them. (This was Flowbase's main problem.)
- Write down every issue and sort it by how much it hurts.
- Use the [technical SEO checklist](/checklists/technical-seo-checklist/) and the [SEO audit checklist](/checklists/seo-audit-checklist/) for the full list.

## Phase 4: Measurement and baseline
**When:** Weeks 1-2.

- Fill in a real test demo form and check it shows up in GA4 and the CRM.
- Write down today's numbers (rankings, traffic, and demos) as a starting point.
- This is how you prove growth in three months.
- Pair it with the [conversion tracking checklist](/checklists/conversion-tracking-checklist/) and the [GA4 audit checklist](/checklists/ga4-audit-checklist/).

## Phase 5: All-pages report and existing potential
**When:** Weeks 2-3.

- Put every page the client already has into one sheet.
- For each page, note its type, funnel stage, main keyword, search volume, and traffic over the last six months.
- Find pages sitting on page two (positions 5 to 20) that could reach page one with a small push. These quick wins come first.
- It is the same page-by-page habit as the [content audit checklist](/checklists/content-audit-checklist/).

## Phase 6: Keyword research and gap analysis
**When:** Weeks 2-4.

- Start with the words buyers actually use.
- Look at what rivals rank for, plus industry terms and integration searches.
- Group them into topics and pick the ones most likely to bring demos.
- If a keyword is owned by a much bigger site, pick an easier, longer version instead. Use the [keyword research checklist](/checklists/keyword-research-checklist/) and a [competitor analysis](/checklists/seo-competitor-analysis-checklist/).

## Phase 7: Strategy and roadmap
**When:** Weeks 3-4.

- Make two lists: fix pages the client already has, and build new ones.
- Score each item by reward versus effort, so the quick wins go first.
- Lay it out on a 90-day plan that names what ships in the first 30, 60, and 90 days.
- Get the client to approve the plan before you build. It mirrors the [SaaS SEO strategy checklist](/checklists/saas-seo-strategy-checklist/).

## Phase 8: Build the money pages first
**When:** Ongoing, from week 4.

- Build the pages people read when they are close to buying: "Flowbase vs Asana", "Asana alternatives", pricing, and integration pages.
- Follow the same loop each time: brief, write, add a clear "book a demo" button, check, publish, then ask Google to index it by hand.
- These bring in demos the fastest. Use the [on-page SEO checklist](/checklists/on-page-seo-checklist/) and the [conversion tracking checklist](/checklists/conversion-tracking-checklist/).

## Phase 9: Build topical authority (TOFU)
**When:** Ongoing.

- Write content that supports the money pages: blog posts, glossary terms, how-to guides, and free tools.
- Give each piece a real author and point the reader on to a money page.
- This builds trust and helps the money pages rank. See the [content SEO checklist](/checklists/content-seo-checklist/).

## Phase 10: Internal linking (TOFU to BOFU)
**When:** Ongoing.

- Link the blog posts and guides to the money pages, so their strength flows down.
- Use clear link words (like "Asana migration guide", not "click here").
- Fix any page with no links pointing to it, and keep important pages close to the homepage. Follow the [internal linking checklist](/checklists/internal-linking-checklist/).

## Phase 11: Off-page and digital PR
**When:** Ongoing.

- Find websites that link to the rivals but not to your client.
- Earn links with guest posts, useful data, and full G2 and Capterra profiles.
- Care about a good topic match, not just big numbers, and stay away from paid link tricks. See the [SaaS link building checklist](/checklists/saas-link-building-checklist/) and the [off-page SEO checklist](/checklists/off-page-seo-checklist/).

## Phase 12: AI search (AEO and GEO)
**When:** Ongoing.

- Let AI bots crawl the site and keep the company facts the same everywhere.
- Write clear answers the AI can quote.
- Each month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews a set of buyer questions and note whether your client or a rival gets named.
- When a rival gets picked and you do not, improve the exact page the AI used. See the [AEO checklist](/checklists/aeo-checklist/) and the [GEO checklist](/checklists/geo-checklist/).

## Phase 13: Reporting and iteration
**When:** Weekly, monthly, quarterly.

- Every week: show traffic and leads split by funnel stage, so you can see what is working.
- Every month: report the five numbers that matter, starting with demos and pipeline, and hold a strategy call.
- Every quarter: change the plan based on what actually worked. Use the [SaaS SEO reporting checklist](/checklists/saas-seo-reporting-checklist/) and the [marketing attribution checklist](/checklists/marketing-attribution-checklist/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an SEO SOP?

An SEO SOP (standard operating procedure) is a written, repeatable set of steps one person follows to turn search into leads. It starts with onboarding and a technical check and ends with weekly reporting. It is different from a strategy because it is in order, so you always know what to do and when. This playbook lays it out in 13 phases built for B2B SaaS.

### How long does each phase take?

The setup phases (onboarding, access, technical audit, tracking, the all-pages report, keyword research, and the plan) take about the first four weeks. The build and growth phases (money pages, supporting content, internal links, off-page, and AI search) are ongoing and start around week four. Reporting runs every week, month, and quarter for as long as the work continues.

### Why build money pages before blog content?

Money pages like "vs", "alternatives", pricing, and integration pages are read by people who are close to buying, so they bring in demos fastest, often within the first 30 days. Building them first means you get leads early instead of waiting months for blog traffic to grow. The blog content comes next to support those pages and keep their rankings safe.

### Is this SOP for one person or a whole team?

It is written as one SEO team member's routine, so a single person can run the whole thing. On a bigger team you split the phases across people (a strategist, an analyst, a content writer, a link builder), but the steps and the order stay exactly the same. Keeping it as one clear sequence is what makes it easy to hand over.

### How is this different from a normal SEO checklist?

A normal SEO checklist gives you tips with no order, and it reads the same for a shop as for a software company. This is a workflow: the steps are in order (check, set up, build, report), every item is a real B2B SaaS action tied to demos, and each phase has a clear time. It tells you what to do, when to do it, and why.

### Can a small in-house team use this, or is it only for agencies?

A small in-house team can use it too. The phases stay the same. You just set a pace that fits your real time (say, six page updates plus four new money pages a month). It scales down cleanly because it is built around order and priority, not team size.

### Where does AI search (AEO and GEO) fit in?

AI search is an ongoing phase that starts once your money pages and blog content exist, because AI tools tend to quote pages that already look trustworthy. The work includes letting AI bots crawl your site, keeping your company facts the same everywhere, writing clear answers AI can quote, and checking each month whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews mention you. When a rival gets named and you do not, you improve the exact page the AI used and check again next month.
