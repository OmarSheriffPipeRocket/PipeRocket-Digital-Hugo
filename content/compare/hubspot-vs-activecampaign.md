short_answer:
  heading: "The short answer"
  intro: >-
    HubSpot is a CRM that runs marketing automation. ActiveCampaign is
    marketing automation that runs a light CRM. HubSpot's Marketing, Sales,
    Service, Content, Operations, and Commerce "Hubs" all write to one central
    CRM object model, so a contact, deal, or ticket is the same record no
    matter which team touches it. ActiveCampaign built the opposite way: its
    automation engine came first, and its CRM ("Pipelines") is a lighter
    add-on layered on top rather than the architectural center. The honest
    split is breadth versus depth-per-dollar. HubSpot's free CRM tier is
    generous, but the feature set most teams actually need (workflow
    automation, custom reporting, ABM tools) is gated behind the Professional
    tier at $800/month on annual billing ($890/month billed monthly), plus a
    one-time $3,000 onboarding fee. ActiveCampaign's
    Starter plan starts at $15/month and unlocks real automation building
    (branching logic, tag-based triggers, goals) well before that price point,
    though it has no comparable ticketing, service-desk, or CMS product.
  callouts:
    - label: "Choose HubSpot"
      title: "One CRM/data layer across marketing, sales, and service"
      body: >-
        If you're a growing B2B team (roughly 10+ people) that needs marketing,

        full go-to-market suite (pipeline, sequences, ticketing, CMS, ABM
        tools) in one system, and can absorb the price step-up past the
        free/Starter tier, HubSpot's breadth is buying real consolidation, from
        a free CRM (up to 2 users) up to $3,600/month for Marketing Hub
        Enterprise.
    - label: "Choose ActiveCampaign"
      title: "Deeper automation and better deliverability at a lower price"

        If you're a solo operator, small e-commerce brand, or email-first SMB
        that needs **granular, behavior-based automation** (tag triggers,
        goals, if/else branching) without a full go-to-market suite, and you
        want a deliverability edge for high-volume sends, ActiveCampaign's
        automation-first architecture is the better fit at $15/month up to
        $145/month Enterprise (both annual billing).

faqs:
  - q: "What is the difference between HubSpot and ActiveCampaign?"
    a: >-
      HubSpot is a CRM that runs marketing automation: its Marketing, Sales,
      Service, Content, Operations, and Commerce Hubs all write to one central
      CRM object model, giving every team the same contact timeline.
      ActiveCampaign is marketing automation that runs a light CRM: its
      automation engine is the architectural center, and its CRM ("Pipelines")
      is an add-on layered on top. HubSpot's real feature set (automation,
      custom reporting, ABM tools) is gated behind its Professional tier at
      $800/month on annual billing; ActiveCampaign unlocks comparable
      automation depth starting around $49/month.
  - q: "Is HubSpot or ActiveCampaign better for small businesses?"
    a: >-
      Neither is better in the abstract. ActiveCampaign is generally the
      stronger fit for solo operators, small e-commerce shops, and email-first
      SMBs that need deep, granular automation without a full go-to-market
      suite, starting at $15-19/month. HubSpot is the stronger fit once a small
      business is growing past roughly 10 people and needs marketing, sales,
      and support to share one system of record, but its real feature set
      starts at $800/month (annual billing) once you're past the free/Starter
      tier.
  - q: "Which is cheaper, HubSpot or ActiveCampaign?"
    a: >-
      ActiveCampaign is cheaper where it counts. HubSpot's Starter is actually
      the cheaper entry point at $7/seat/month on annual billing (a
      promotional rate against a $20/seat/month list price) versus
      ActiveCampaign's $15/month, but the gap reverses sharply above that:
      ActiveCampaign unlocks unlimited automation and predictive emailing on
      its $49/month Plus tier, well below HubSpot's $800/month Marketing Hub
      Professional tier where equivalent automation depth becomes available.
      HubSpot also carries mandatory one-time onboarding fees ($3,000 at
      Marketing Hub Professional, $7,000 at Enterprise) that ActiveCampaign
      does not publish as a standard charge.
  - q: "Which platform has better email automation?"
    a: >-
      ActiveCampaign is widely cited in third-party comparisons as the deeper,
      more flexible automation builder, with tag-based triggers, goals,
      branching if/else logic, and site/event tracking available at a lower
      price point than HubSpot's equivalent tier. It's also repeatedly noted
      as having a deliverability edge for high-volume email use cases like
      e-commerce and newsletters. HubSpot's workflow automation is capable but
      gated behind its Professional tier ($800/month annual) and is generally
      regarded as less granular for pure email/behavioral sequencing.
  - q: "Does either platform have a free plan?"
    a: >-
      HubSpot has a genuine free-forever CRM tier with no time limit, covering
      core CRM features for up to two users. ActiveCampaign does not offer a
      free-forever plan; it offers a 14-day free trial (plus a 30-day
      money-back guarantee) across all paid tiers instead. If a permanent free
      tier matters to your evaluation, HubSpot is the only one of the two that
      offers it.
  - q: "Which has the better G2 or Capterra rating?"
    a: >-
      ActiveCampaign rates marginally higher on both platforms. G2 reports
      4.5/5 across 14,000+ reviews for ActiveCampaign versus 4.4/5 across
      35,766 reviews for HubSpot's seller-page aggregate, and Capterra shows
      4.6/5 (2,568 reviews) for ActiveCampaign versus 4.5/5 (4,477 reviews)
      for HubSpot CRM. The gap is small, and "HubSpot" spans several
      separately-rated products (Marketing Hub 4.5/5 from 6,248 Capterra
      reviews, Sales Hub 4.5/5 from 503), so the single cleanest
      apples-to-apples HubSpot figure is its CRM Capterra score. HubSpot's
      review base is also far larger on both platforms, which makes its
      slightly lower score the more heavily sampled one.
  - q: "Can HubSpot and ActiveCampaign be used together?"
    a: >-
      They serve different architectural roles, so pairing them is unusual but
      not unheard of. Most buyers choose one as their primary system of record
      rather than running both, since HubSpot's CRM and ActiveCampaign's
      Pipelines CRM would otherwise compete for the same contact data. Teams
      that need HubSpot's sales/service breadth alongside ActiveCampaign-style
      granular automation typically evaluate whether HubSpot's own workflow
      builder (once on Professional) covers their needs before adding a second
      automation tool.

## Strengths & tradeoffs

Both platforms do the core job of managing contacts and sending automated marketing competently. The real differences are architecture, automation depth, and price, and each side wins rows the other does not.
