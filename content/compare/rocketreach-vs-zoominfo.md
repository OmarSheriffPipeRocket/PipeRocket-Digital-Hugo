    publicly traded (Nasdaq: GTM). Both carry real, documented user
    complaints, which are flagged plainly below rather than smoothed over.

## Strengths & tradeoffs

Both tools solve the contact-lookup problem competently, but they are built
for different buyers and different budgets. Each side wins rows the other
does not, and this is a neutral comparison, not a case for either vendor.

| Axis | RocketReach | ZoomInfo |
|---|---|---|
| **Pricing model** | Self-serve, published per-seat tiers (~$19-300/seat/mo) | Sales-led annual contract, no public price list, median ~$33,500/yr |
| **Data breadth** | 700M+ contact profiles and 60M+ companies claimed; thinner firmographics | 300M+ contact profiles, 100M+ company profiles, deeper firmographics |
| **Intent data** | Not offered | Core product strength (210M+ IP-to-org pairings claimed) |
| **Workflow automation** | Integrations and email verification only; Messages and Autopilot add light email engagement | OperationsOS (enrichment, dedup, CRM hygiene) plus Copilot AI |
| **Speed/simplicity** | Real-time point-of-lookup verification, browser extension | Positioned as one layer of a broader platform, more setup |
| **G2 data-accuracy attribute** | 80/100 (~280 reviews, third-party aggregation) | 77/100 (~4,000+ reviews, same aggregation) |
| **Documented complaints** | Outdated contacts, 20-30% bounce rates reported, AI-only support | Opaque high-cost pricing, 3-seat minimum, some reviewers report inconsistent intent data |
| **Review-base size** | Smaller footprint on both G2 and Capterra | Far larger G2 and Capterra review base |
| **Contract flexibility** | No seat minimum on self-serve tiers | 3-seat minimum reportedly enforced on every tier |
| **Best-fit buyer** | Lean sales/recruiting/BD teams, individual prospectors | Mid-market to enterprise revenue teams with a mature RevOps stack |

numbers without converting the scale. On the
data-accuracy attribute specifically, RocketReach edges ahead at 80/100
