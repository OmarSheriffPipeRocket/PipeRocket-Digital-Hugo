    Originality.ai and Copyleaks are both AI-content and plagiarism detectors,
    but they were built for different buyers. Originality.ai is the younger

    academic-integrity reputation. Both vendors publish internal accuracy

    for either tool, which is a category-wide pattern rather than a flaw
    unique to one vendor.

faqs:
  - q: "What is the difference between Originality.ai and Copyleaks?"
    a: >-
      Originality.ai is a younger tool (founded November 2022) built for
      content agencies and publishers, bundling AI detection, plagiarism
      checking, grammar, readability, fact-checking, and full-site scans in
      one product, with far more words included per dollar at the entry tier.
      Copyleaks is older (founded 2015), with native integration across eight
      learning management systems, plagiarism scanning it describes as
      reading over 100 languages, AI detection stated for 30+ languages, and
      a distinct code-detection product (Codeleaks). The right pick depends on
      whether you need a bundled content-QA suite or education/LMS and
      multilingual reach.
  - q: "Which is more accurate, Originality.ai or Copyleaks?"
    a: >-
      Independent third-party benchmarks put both tools in a similar range,
      and neither reproduces its own marketing claim under outside testing.
      The one benchmark that tested both side by side, GPTZero's RAID run
      across 3,000 samples, scored Copyleaks higher at 90.7% accuracy
      (5.26% false positives) versus Originality.ai at 83.0% (4.79% false
      positives). Other single-tool tests land differently: EyeSift measured
      Originality.ai at 88% with a 5.7% false-positive rate, while Supwriter
      measured Copyleaks at 77.5% across 200+ samples and Leap AI's
      qualitative review placed Copyleaks behind Originality.ai, Turnitin,
      and Winston on standard prose without publishing comparable figures.
      Copyleaks claims over 99% accuracy with a 0.03% false-positive rate and
      Originality.ai claims 99%+, gaps from independent results that show up
      industry-wide rather than at one vendor. Treat any single number as
      directional and run your own sample test before committing.
