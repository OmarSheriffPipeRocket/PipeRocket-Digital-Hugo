  intro: >-
    Serpstat and Moz Pro both cover the core SEO workflow (keyword research,
    backlink analysis, rank tracking, and site audit), but they were built for
    different budgets and different jobs. Serpstat is the more affordable
    all-in-one option once you account for how much a plan actually unlocks
    (unlimited projects and API access on its Team tier), and it is the only
    one of the two with a native PPC competitive-research module. Moz Pro
    carries two decades of brand history, a much larger public review and
    case-study trail, and keeps its full feature set available on every
    tier, even the cheapest one.

faqs:
  - q: "What is the difference between Serpstat and Moz Pro?"
    a: >-
      Serpstat is an all-in-one SEO and PPC research platform: alongside
      keyword research, backlink analysis, rank tracking, and site audit, it
      includes a native paid-search competitive module Moz Pro does not
      have. Moz Pro is SEO-only but keeps its full feature set (Keyword
      Explorer, Link Explorer, Site Crawl, On-Page Grader) available on
      every tier, including its cheapest. Serpstat gates some features
      (API access, white-label reporting) behind higher tiers instead.
  - q: "Is Serpstat cheaper than Moz Pro?"
    a: >-
      At the entry tier, Moz Pro is nominally cheaper ($39/mo annual Starter
      versus Serpstat's $50/mo annual Individual). But Serpstat's Team plan
      ($100/mo annual) includes unlimited projects and API access, while
      Moz's comparable Standard tier ($79/mo annual) caps out at 10
      campaigns and 1,500 tracked keywords. For teams that need to scale
      project count without jumping to the top tier, Serpstat is reported to
      offer more capability per dollar. Verify both prices live before
      budgeting.
  - q: "Which tool has better reviews, Serpstat or Moz Pro?"
    a: >-
      Serpstat holds a higher average rating on both G2 (4.6/5 versus Moz's
      4.3/5) and Capterra (4.7/5 versus Moz's 4.5/5), though both G2 figures
      should be verified live since automated fetches were blocked during
      this research. Moz Pro has a much larger review base (605 G2 reviews,
      350 Capterra reviews, plus a FeaturedCustomers composite of 4.8/5
      across 1,756 reference ratings) reflecting over two decades in market,
      versus Serpstat's roughly 462 G2 and under 200 Capterra reviews. A
      higher average on a smaller sample and a lower average on a much
      larger sample are both worth weighing, not just the headline number.
  - q: "Does Serpstat or Moz Pro have PPC research features?"
    a: >-
      Serpstat includes a native PPC competitive-research module covering ad
      copy, keyword, and budget insight for paid search, built into its core
      product. Moz Pro has no equivalent paid-search feature. If your team
      runs SEO and PPC together and wants one tool for competitive research
      across both, that is a point in Serpstat's favor.
  - q: "Which tool has stronger case studies and named clients?"
    a: >-
      Moz Pro publishes labeled, quantified customer case studies (for
      example, a Wpromote-led PAPYRUS engagement reporting an 82 percent
      increase in listing views) through its FeaturedCustomers profile,
      alongside named clients like Beacon, Coalmarch, and TopSpot. Serpstat's
      public clients page displays customer logos without naming most of
      them, and only one named agency case study (Vintage Web Production)
      surfaced in this research. Teams that weigh vendor credibility on
      documented outcomes will find more to evaluate on Moz's side.
  - q: "Which tool is easier to get full functionality from on a lower budget?"
    a: >-
      Moz Pro. Every tier, including its cheapest Starter plan, includes the
      same full feature set (Keyword Explorer, Link Explorer, Site Crawl,
      On-Page Grader); only data limits like campaigns and tracked keywords
      change as you move up. Serpstat's lower tiers work but withhold real
      functionality, including API access, white-label reporting, and phone
      support, until you reach its Team or Agency plans. If budget is fixed
      and feature completeness matters more than data volume, Moz Pro's
      structure is the more predictable one.

## Strengths & tradeoffs

Both platforms handle the core SEO workflow well. The honest differences show up in pricing structure, PPC coverage, brand track record, and how much of the toolset you get at the entry price.

| Axis | Serpstat | Moz Pro |
|---|---|---|
| **PPC research** | Native ad copy, keyword, and budget competitive module | No equivalent feature |
| **Entry pricing** | $50/mo (Individual, annual); scaling to $100/mo unlocks unlimited projects + API | $39/mo (Starter, annual); full features included, limits scale with tier |
| **Plan structure** | Feature-gated: API, white-label, and phone support locked to higher tiers | Feature-parity: every tier gets the same tools, only limits change |
| **Rating (G2)** | 4.6/5, 462 reviews (verify live) | 4.3/5, 605 reviews (verify live) |
| **Rating (Capterra)** | 4.7/5, roughly 169 to 170 reviews, a comparatively small sample | 4.5/5, 350 reviews |
| **Case studies** | Unlabeled client logos; one named agency case study | Multiple named, quantified case studies via FeaturedCustomers |
| **Brand history** | Founded 2013, standalone circa 2015 to 2016; smaller footprint outside Eastern Europe budget-SEO circles | Founded 2004; Rand Fishkin's Beginner's Guide to SEO gave it lasting industry influence |
| **On-page tooling** | No named dedicated grader in this research pass | On-Page Grader, named and distinct capability |
| **Support** | Reviewers repeatedly cite fast, high-quality support (reported G2 support sub-score 9.4) | No equivalent standout support sub-score surfaced |
| **UI** | Reported as data-dense, can feel overwhelming to new users | Reported as cleaner and more beginner-friendly |
