*This case study covers the first three quarters (Jan–Sep 2025) of an ongoing engagement — a progress snapshot, not a full-year result.*

## The challenge

Atlas Systems sells into a specialised, high-stakes enterprise category — third-party risk management (TPRM) and provider lifecycle management (PLM) — where buyers research compliance and vendor-risk software long before they ever talk to sales.

At the start of the engagement, Atlas had almost no organic visibility to meet that research: an average keyword position in the high 30s, a handful of Top-3 rankings, and negligible presence in the emerging channel of AI-assisted search (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini). The challenge wasn't one product to rank for — it was **two distinct buyer categories** (TPRM and PLM) that each needed their own keyword coverage, content depth, and authority, built from close to zero at the same time.

## Our approach

The strategy, in the team's own words: **"capture existing market demand in intent channels and create new demand in awareness channels."** In practice, that meant building two parallel content tracks under each product line:

- **ToFU (awareness) content** — educational, top-of-funnel material establishing Atlas as a source on TPRM and PLM topics broadly, not just its own product.
- **BoFU (bottom-funnel) content** — solution and comparison pages aimed directly at in-market buyers already evaluating vendors.

On top of that foundation:

- **Publishing velocity was scaled aggressively** — from dozens of pieces approved in Q1 to a 140+ page target entering Q2, backed by a live content-status tracker to keep production honest against plan.
- **Technical SEO issues were fixed on an ongoing basis**, clearing the structural blockers that cap how far new content can rank.
- **Authority building ran in parallel** — targeted high-quality referring-domain acquisition alongside the content push.
- **Visibility was pursued beyond traditional search** — YouTube video, programmatic SEO, free interactive tools, and community engagement (Reddit, Quora, StackOverflow) were all built into the plan to diversify how buyers could discover Atlas.
- **AI-search visibility was treated as a first-class channel from day one** — tracking and optimising for citation in ChatGPT and Perplexity answers starting in Q1, well ahead of most competitors treating this as a real channel.

## The results

Every headline metric moved in the same direction, every quarter, with no reversals across the first three quarters:

| Metric | Q1 '25 | Q2 '25 | Q3 '25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Non-branded impressions (GSC) | 1.20M | 3.38M | 5.86M |
| Non-branded clicks (GSC) | 8,450 | 9,280 | 12,200 |
| Avg. keyword position | 39.5 | 41.2 | 35.8 |
| ToFU content clicks | 455 | 1,637 | 5,685 |
| BoFU content clicks | 232 | 1,257 | 1,692 |
| Organic leads | 3 | 12 | 14 |
| MQLs | 0 | 4 | 8 |
| Top-3 keyword rankings | 76 | 109 | 180 |
| AI-search referral sessions | 219 | 732 | 956 |
| Bounce rate | 59.3% (Jan) | — | 33.9% (Sep) |

**Reading the trend:** content traffic scaled the fastest of any metric — ToFU clicks alone grew roughly 12.5x — while rankings and impressions climbed in step, confirming the growth was coming from genuinely new visibility, not a handful of outlier pages. AI-search referral sessions grew faster than traditional organic clicks (+337% vs. +44%), an early signal that the investment in LLM-citation visibility is paying off ahead of the broader market catching on. Bounce rate falling from 59.3% to 33.9% points to the content getting more relevant to the searches landing on it, not just more voluminous.

## Why it worked

1. **Two categories, two tracks, one system.** Building ToFU and BoFU content separately for TPRM and PLM meant neither buyer category diluted the other's keyword signal — each built its own authority in parallel.
2. **Volume was paired with a tracker, not left to chance.** Scaling from a Q1 backlog to a 140+ page Q2 target only works if production is actively measured against plan — which it was, every week.
3. **AI-search was a Day-1 channel, not an afterthought.** Most organic programs are still catching up to ChatGPT and Perplexity as a source of traffic. Atlas was already tracking and building for it in Q1 — which is likely why its AI-referral growth outpaced traditional search growth.
4. **Diversification reduced single-channel risk.** Video, programmatic SEO, free tools, and community answers gave the program more than one lever to pull as it matured.

## Honest notes

- **This covers Q1–Q3 2025 only** — the first three quarters of an ongoing engagement, not a full-year or final result.
- **Lead and MQL volumes are still small in absolute terms** (single digits to low teens per quarter), so the percentage growth, while real, is off a low base.
- **Avg. position and rankings reflect GSC-tracked queries** as reported at the time; both continued to be actively worked on beyond Q3.
